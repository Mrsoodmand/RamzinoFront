# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Primary: prospective and existing Ramzino merchants — Iranian website owners evaluating or already running a crypto payment gateway. Prospective merchants arrive comparing fees, supported currencies, and integration effort before they trust Ramzino with revenue; existing merchants return for docs, the API reference, and fee details. Persian is the first language and RTL the native direction; English/LTR is a secondary locale, mirrored rather than translated.

Secondary: general crypto-curious visitors reading blog content or checking currency info, and end-payers researching Ramzino before completing a hosted checkout.

## Product Purpose

RamzinoFront is the public face of Ramzino, a multi-tenant crypto payment-gateway platform for the Iranian market. It exists to educate and convert: explain what the gateway does, what it costs, which currencies it supports, and how integration works, clearly enough that a visitor trusts Ramzino and signs up or logs in. Success is a visitor's doubts being resolved on this site, not carried unanswered into the sign-up form.

## Positioning

The full-service platform: more than a gateway — balances, withdrawals, support desk, donations/tip-jar, one panel for a merchant's entire crypto operation. The public site's job is to make that claim credible before the merchant ever logs in.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: register / create a payment gateway. Secondary fallback: explore first — browse fees, supported currencies, or docs before committing.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: this is a full-service crypto payment platform for merchants, not just a gateway — and it's built to feel safe with their revenue.
- Belief ladder: (1) Ramzino supports the currencies and customers I need → (2) fees are transparent and fair → (3) my funds and KYC are handled safely and can be withdrawn on demand → (4) integration is straightforward (docs and API reference exist and are clear) → (5) support exists if something goes wrong.
- Proof on hand: none yet.

## Brand Personality

Trustworthy, calm, precise — the same financial-infrastructure feel as the merchant panel, carried onto the public face. Quiet surfaces, clear numbers, no drama. The site is selling trust, not excitement, so restraint itself is the persuasion.

## Anti-references

Crypto-casino flash: neon gradients, coin animations, hype charts, moon/rocket energy — the meme-exchange aesthetic that erodes financial trust. This is the first page a prospective merchant sees; nothing on it should feel speculative or loud, even in service of conversion.

## Design Principles

1. **Money looks calm, even while selling.** Trust is earned through restraint, not hype — no crypto-casino flash, no speculative energy, ever, regardless of conversion pressure.
2. **Explain, then convert.** Every informational page (fees, currencies, docs, FAQ) exists to remove one doubt before asking for the sign-up; conversion is a byproduct of clarity, not a separate layer bolted on top.
3. **Docs are a sales surface.** The API reference, docs, and FAQ are read by merchants evaluating integration effort — clarity there is itself marketing, not an afterthought.
4. **Persian-first, mirrored — not translated.** RTL is the native direction, not an adaptation. Layouts use logical properties and must genuinely mirror in the English/LTR locale, including icons, numbers, and directional affordances.
5. **One brand, two surfaces.** The public site and the merchant panel are a single trust relationship; visual and voice consistency between them compounds credibility rather than resetting it at login.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No special requirements stated. Working baseline: WCAG AA contrast, full keyboard navigability, `prefers-reduced-motion` alternatives for all animation, and correct bidirectional behavior (Persian RTL / English LTR) throughout.
